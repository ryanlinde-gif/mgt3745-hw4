## Hard Constraints

Three constraints are pass/fail. An option that fails one is rejected before it is
scored, because averaging a disqualifying flaw against favorable criteria is how a
weighted table launders a bad decision.

1. **Zero recurring cost.** Not "cheap." Zero. The business has no software budget.
2. **No athlete data held where another family's data is co-mingled or where I
   cannot delete it on request.** This comes from `FEATURES.md`: "No athlete data
   is published or shared between families." The subject is a minor.
3. **Working by 2026-09-17.**

**Rejected before scoring.** Commercial recruiting CRMs such as SportsRecruits and
similar platforms fail constraint 1 on recurring fee alone. Any option requiring a
paid hosting tier to persist data fails the same constraint.

**Three options survive to scoring:**

- **Hand-built option (Build).** Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, adapted from the
  course starter, persisting to browser localStorage.
- **Existing-service option (Buy).** A free Google Sheet or Airtable base the
  athlete fills in, with a formula column for the follow-up flag.
- **AI-assisted build (Delegate).** The same three files, generated by an agent
  from the spec, reviewed and committed by me.

Each option is scored at its most favorable honest reading. The Buy option in
particular is scored as a free spreadsheet rather than as a paid CRM, because
scoring an option at its worst is the same error as scoring your preferred option
at its best.

## Gate

Weights were assigned before any option was scored. Scores run 1 to 5, where 5 is
always most favorable.

### Anchors

| Criterion | 1 means | 3 means | 5 means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost to start | Recurring fee required | One-time cost or a free tier with real limits | Free with no ceiling that this feature reaches |
| Cost to maintain | I must pay or re-learn it to keep it running | Occasional upkeep I can perform | Someone else maintains it, or there is almost nothing to maintain |
| Time to working | More than a week | Two to four days | Under one day |
| Inspectability | I cannot see or check how it behaves | I can see the data but not verify the behavior | I can read every line and confirm it matches the spec |
| Switching cost | Data is trapped or must be re-entered by hand | Export exists, logic must be rebuilt | Data is plain text and logic is portable |
| Fit to spec | Cannot express the EARS statements at all | Stores the data, cannot enforce the behavior | Implements the EARS statements directly |

### Table

| Criterion | Weight | Hand-built | Existing-service | AI-assisted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cost to start | 5 | 5 (25) | 5 (25) | 5 (25) |
| Cost to maintain | 3 | 3 (9) | 5 (15) | 2 (6) |
| Time to working | 4 | 2 (8) | 5 (20) | 5 (20) |
| Inspectability | 5 | 5 (25) | 3 (15) | 1 (5) |
| Switching cost | 2 | 4 (8) | 3 (6) | 4 (8) |

Maximum possible is 120. The spread between first and third is 16 points out of
120, which is narrow enough that the sensitivity check below is doing real work
rather than confirming a foregone conclusion.

### Why the weights are what they are

**Cost to start, 5.** The budget is zero, so this is the criterion most able to
disqualify. It ends up discriminating between none of the three survivors, which
is itself informative: the hard constraint already did that work.

**Cost to maintain, 3.** I am the only person who will touch this, which argues
high. But the feature is small and Module 4 replaces the storage layer anyway, so
the maintenance window is weeks, not years. Middle.

**Time to working, 4.** Two days, alongside four other courses and a business.
High, but deliberately not 5, because something that ships on time and cannot be
checked is worth less to me than something that ships late and can be.

**Inspectability, 5.** This decides the table, and it decides it because of a fact
about me rather than a fact about the technology. **I cannot yet read JavaScript.**
I can follow the shape of a file, but I could not distinguish correct code from
code that merely runs without throwing. Weighting this at 5 is not caution for its
own sake; an unverifiable result is not a result.

**Switching cost, 2.** Lowest, deliberately. Module 4 introduces a database, so
this storage decision has a known expiry date. Optimizing against lock-in for a
component I already plan to replace is spending attention on a problem that
resolves itself.

**Fit to spec, 5.** The spec is unusually specific. E13 names a seven-day
threshold, E12 requires naming the missing field, and E5 requires the athlete see
every coach contacted on her behalf. Generic tools do not know any of that.

### Why the scores are what they are

**All three score 5 on cost to start,** because the hard constraint already
removed everything that did not. This row separates nothing and I am leaving it in
rather than deleting it, because a criterion that failed to discriminate is a
finding about the criterion.

**Existing-service scores 5 on maintenance and time to working, and 2 on fit.**
Google maintains the sheet and it works in twenty minutes; both are true and I am
not going to pretend otherwise. It loses on fit for one reason: **a spreadsheet
stores, it does not behave.** E12 requires rejecting an entry and naming the field
that is missing. E13 requires marking an entry overdue. Data validation can block
a cell, but it cannot produce the behavior the EARS statements describe. Its 3 on
inspectability is honest in a way worth stating: every value is visible, there is
simply no logic to verify.

**Hand-built scores 5 on inspectability** for the only reason that score is ever
honest: I wrote it, so I understand it. That is circular for an experienced
developer and load-bearing for me. It scores 2 on time to working, its worst cell,
because I am learning while building.

**AI-assisted scores 5 on time and 1 on inspectability.** Both true at once, and
the gap between them is the entire finding. An agent produces a working contact log
faster than I will, and I would have no way to tell whether it matches the spec,
handles an empty field, or stores data somewhere I did not intend. Its 2 on
maintenance is the lowest cell in the table: code I cannot read is code I cannot
fix, so the maintenance cost is not the agent's time but my dependence on it.

## Sensitivity Check

The gate favored Hand-built by 9 points over Existing-service. That is 7.5% of the
scale, close enough that I tested whether the result survives disagreement.

**Test 1: lower the weight I am least sure of.** Inspectability at 5 is the most
subjective weight in the table and the one another person would most plausibly
dispute. Dropping it from 5 to 3:

| Option | Original | Inspectability weighted 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Hand-built | 100 | 90 |
| Existing-service | 91 | 85 |
| AI-assisted | 84 | 82 |

The order holds and the margin narrows from 9 to 5. **The decision is not
sensitive to that weight.** It would take dropping inspectability to a weight of 1
for Existing-service to win, and at weight 1 I would be asserting that being able
to check my own work barely matters, which I do not believe.

**Test 2: change the score that rests on a fact about me.** AI-assisted scores 1
on inspectability because I cannot read JavaScript today. That is not permanent.

| AI-assisted inspectability score | AI-assisted total | Winner |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 (today) | 84 | Hand-built, 100 |
| 3 | 94 | Hand-built, 100 |
| 4 | 99 | Hand-built, 100 |
| 5 | 104 | **AI-assisted** |

**This is the finding.** The decision does not flip on any weight I might have
gotten wrong. It flips on one thing: whether I can read the code well enough to
audit it. At a score of 5 the AI-assisted option wins outright, and nothing else in
the table has to change.

That makes my own literacy the revisit trigger, not the calendar, and it means the
week I spend hand-building is not a detour from the better option. It is the thing
that unlocks it.

## ADR-001

**Title and date:** ADR-001 — Adapt the course starter by hand and persist the
contact log in browser localStorage. 2026-09-15.

**Status:** Accepted.

**Door / concrete acquisition and execution choice:** Build (hand-built option).
Concretely: fork the course starter's three root files, rewrite the note model as a
contact entry with coach, school, date, and status, keep the existing load, save,
render structure, and add the seven-day overdue rule. No new dependencies, no
hosting, no account.

**Context.** The athlete in `USERS.md` (Profile B, my sister, class of 2029) does
her own coach outreach and loses track of who she has contacted. Her words: she
tells herself she needs to follow up, then "practice or school or something else"
happens and she forgets which coaches she was supposed to email again. The failure
is memory, not motivation, so the fix is a record rather than a reminder.

Four forces act. The **spec** is already written and specific: E5 commits to showing
her the coach, the date, and the follow-up status, and the constraints forbid
sharing athlete data between families. My **skills** are the binding constraint; I
cannot read JavaScript, so every door but Build yields an artifact I would have to
trust rather than check, and this course grades attestation. The **budget** is zero,
which the hard constraints already resolved. The **deadline** is two days, and it is
the one force pointing away from Build. I am accepting a slower path deliberately.

One tension in my own spec, named rather than hidden: `FEATURES.md` lists "Email
only; no app or login" as a constraint, and this decision builds a web page. That
constraint governs **delivery of the Reality Check**, which stays email. The contact
log is the athlete's own working tool on her own device, not a portal a family logs
into to receive a deliverable. The constraint survives; its scope needed stating,
and `FEATURES.md` now states it.

**Decision.** I will adapt the course starter by hand into a contact log persisted
in browser localStorage, keeping structure in `index.html`, presentation in
`styles.css`, and behavior and data in `app.js`, with no external dependencies,
because the weighted gate favored the hand-built option at 100 against 91 and 84,
the result survived a sensitivity test on the weight I was least sure of, and the
one condition that would reverse it is a skill I do not yet have.

**Consequences and revisit trigger.**

*Easier.* I can verify my own EARS statements against something I understand rather
than reporting that a page appeared to work. Every line is changeable by me without
a round trip. The data is plain JSON, so moving it into whatever Module 4 brings is
a read and a reshape, not a migration.

*Harder.* It is the slowest surviving option. I own every bug. And "I wrote it so I
understand it" holds for the logic I intended, not necessarily for the mistakes I
made, which is a weaker claim than it sounds.

*What this decision fails to do.* localStorage is per-browser and per-device. If my
sister logs contacts on her laptop, her phone shows an empty list. There is no sync
and no backup, and clearing site data destroys the log with no warning and no
recovery. For a user whose entire complaint is losing track of things, shipping
storage that can silently vanish is a real defect, not a rough edge. It also cannot
satisfy E5's parent-facing counterpart at all, because the parent is on a different
device by definition. Single-user, single-device is accepted for this module and
disqualifying for the product.

*Revisit trigger.* Two conditions, either one sufficient. **One:** I can read and
audit JavaScript, at which point Test 2 above says the AI-assisted option wins and
this record should be reconsidered on its own terms. **Two:** Module 4 introduces a
database, at which point the per-device limitation stops being acceptable and
ADR-002 must address sync, the parent's view, and migration of data already sitting
in a browser. Whichever arrives first triggers the review. This record is not
deleted when that happens; it is marked Superseded, because the reasoning was
correct on the date it was written.

## Note on the Pedagogical Build and the Recommendation

The course requires a browser build this week regardless of what the gate concluded,
so it is worth stating plainly whether my ADR is a real recommendation or a
rationalization of a required exercise.

In my case they agree, and the agreement is not a coincidence. The gate turned on
inspectability, the required build exists precisely because attestation requires
literacy, and both point the same direction for the same reason. Had the gate
returned Existing-service, the honest write-up would have been that the ADR
recommends a spreadsheet and the build is a teaching exercise. The sensitivity check
is what keeps this distinction verifiable: it shows the result was tested against
disagreement rather than steered toward the assignment.
