## 1. Candidate Problem

I run a college soccer recruiting business that helps high school athletes with
the recruiting process, and one problem I have is turning interested athletes
into paying clients. I can get athletes to respond and show interest in getting
recruiting help, but getting from that initial interest to speaking with their
parents and eventually becoming a paying client has been difficult.

## 2. Wickedness Test

I think this problem has several characteristics of a wicked problem. First, it
is hard to formulate one clear problem statement because there are several ways I
could explain what is going wrong. I could say that I am not getting enough
clients, my sales process is not effective enough, parents are not involved early
enough, or athletes do not see enough value to take the next step. There is also
no real stopping rule because even if I improve the percentage of athletes who
become clients, I could always improve the process further. The problem also has
multiple valid explanations because an athlete might not become a client due to
price, lack of urgency, their parents not seeing the value, choosing another
recruiting service, or something I am doing wrong in my process. Finally, I am
liable for the consequences of the decisions I make. For example, contacting
parents earlier might help me get more clients, but it could also come across as
too aggressive and cause some families to lose interest.

**Verdict.** I believe this is a wicked problem because there is no single
explanation or correct solution. Different ways of framing the problem could lead
me toward completely different solutions, and I have to make decisions without
knowing for certain which approach will work best.

## 3. Three Framings

**Framing A (the obvious one).** My business is not converting enough interested
student athletes into paying clients. This perspective shows the problem is
mainly my sales process and figuring out how to get more interested athletes to
take the next step and become clients.

**Framing B (stakeholder shift).** From the parent's perspective, the problem
could be that they do not have enough information or trust in my recruiting
service to feel comfortable paying for it. An athlete might be interested in
getting help, but if their parents do not understand what they are paying for or
why it would help their child, the conversation may never go any further.

**Framing C (goal shift).** The real goal is not just getting more athletes onto
sales calls or convincing more people to pay. The problem could instead be
creating a process where athletes and their families understand the value of
getting recruiting help and feel that continuing to work with me is worth it.

## 4. Commit and Justify

**Chosen framing:** Framing C, the goal shift.

**Why.** I think Framing C gets closer to what I actually want to accomplish with
the business. I could focus completely on getting more people onto calls or
writing better sales messages, but that does not necessarily solve why families
are not moving forward in the first place. If athletes and parents already
understand the value of what I provide before I ask them to become a client, I
think the sales process would become much easier. It also gives me more possible
ways to approach the problem instead of assuming that I just need to become
better at selling.

**What this framing makes invisible.** This framing could make problems with my
actual sales process less visible. Even if families understand the value of the
service, I still have to clearly explain the price, answer their questions,
follow up at the right time, and actually ask them to become a client. If I focus
too much on providing value before the sale, I could overlook problems that are
happening later in the process.

## 5. Oath Check

**The tool I already assumed.** Social media direct messaging, especially using
platforms like X and Instagram to contact student-athletes.

**What would have to be true for it to be justified.** Social media DMs would be
justified if the athletes I am trying to reach are actually active on those
platforms, consistently see and respond to my messages, and those conversations
eventually lead to serious recruiting conversations and clients. If another
method like email, referrals, content, or reaching parents directly produces
better results, then I should be willing to use that instead of assuming DMs are
the best choice just because they are what I already use.

## 6. Adequacy Sketch

A good enough outcome for now would be having a process where a consistent
percentage of interested athletes move from the first conversation into a serious
conversation involving their parents and eventually become clients. I would know
I had reached that point by tracking how many athletes respond, how many move
forward to serious conversations or calls, and how many of those families
eventually become paying clients.

## How This Framing Carried Forward

Framing C survived HW2 and was sharpened by the interviews recorded in
`USERS.md`. The block is not that families fail to understand value in the
abstract. It is that they cannot get evidence of interest before they spend. That
sharpened framing is what `FEATURES.md` specifies against.
